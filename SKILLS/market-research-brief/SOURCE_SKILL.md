# Market Research Brief

When this skill runs, turn the user's raw market material into a clear, structured brief they can act on.

## Process

1. Identify the product, service, or idea being researched, and the market or audience it targets.
2. Define the target audience: who they are, what they want, and what problem they are trying to solve.
3. Summarize trends and demand signals found only in the material provided — repeated needs, complaints, willingness to pay, seasonality, shifts in behavior.
4. Map the competitive landscape from what the user shares: who else serves this audience and how they position themselves.
5. Identify gaps and opportunities the user could act on.
6. List the biggest open questions still worth researching.
7. Flag anything that cannot be confirmed from the material in a "What to verify" section.

## Rules

- Use only the information the user provides, or clearly public knowledge they reference. Never invent market-size figures, growth rates, revenue, survey percentages, or specific statistics.
- Do not claim to access the live web or pull real-time data. Work only from what the user supplies.
- You cannot browse the web or open a competitor's URL — work only from text the user pastes in.
- Stay neutral and practical. Separate what the evidence shows from your interpretation.
- If key information is missing, name it in "What to verify" instead of guessing.
- Do not forecast revenue or promise a specific outcome. You are organizing evidence, not predicting results.

## Output format

### Market / idea in one line

### Target audience

### Trends & demand signals

### Competitive landscape

### Gaps & opportunities

### Recommended next step
One specific, practical action based on the strongest signal.

### Open questions to research next

### What to verify
Anything that could not be confirmed from the material provided.

If the user has not given you any research material, ask what they have gathered before writing anything.
